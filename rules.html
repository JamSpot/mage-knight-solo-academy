Combat: resolve the four phases in order

Combat with enemies begins by revealing all enemies you must fight. Then resolve the four phases below in order. Read every enemy token first: abilities, resistances, fortification and special attack types can change the arithmetic.

  1. Ranged and Siege Attack. Use legal ranged and/or siege attacks to defeat enemies before they can attack. In this phase, fortifications restrict which enemies can be targeted: siege attacks can target fortified enemies, but ranged attacks cannot; an enemy fortified twice cannot be targeted in this phase at all. Total attack must meet or exceed the targeted enemies' total Armour or the attack has no effect.
  2. Block. For each block, choose one attacking enemy. Your block must equal or exceed that enemy's Attack value to stop it completely. A partial block does not reduce the damage. Swift enemies have their Attack doubled for blocking. Against elemental attacks, some block types are inefficient and count at half value.
  3. Assign Damage. Each enemy that was neither defeated nor blocked deals its Attack damage. Assign damage according to the rules for your hero and units; Armour reduces the damage absorbed by each wound, but Armour does not simply cancel an attack before damage is assigned. Resolve enemy abilities that alter damage or wounds.
  4. Attack. Use remaining attacks to defeat enemies. In this phase, regular, ranged and siege attacks can be combined and fortifications no longer restrict targets. Non-Wound cards may be played sideways for physical Attack 1. As before, attack value must meet or exceed the target's Armour.

Combat arithmetic that prevents common mistakes

  • Attack and Block are not interchangeable. Attacks compare against Armour; blocks compare against an enemy's Attack value.
  • Partial Block means no reduction. A Block total of 4 against an Attack of 5 does not reduce the damage to 1; the enemy's attack gets through at full strength.
  • Resistance changes attack value. If a targeted enemy resists an attack type, that matching attack value is inefficient and is halved (total the inefficient attacks first, then halve and round down). Sometimes it is better to split enemies into separate attacks.
  • Defeated enemies leave combat immediately. Discard their tokens and gain the Fame shown; do not let them attack or deal damage later in the combat.
  • Do not forget the order. Ranged/Siege → Block → Assign Damage → Attack. You cannot use a later phase's attacks to prevent damage already assigned in the previous phase.

Worked example: two ordinary enemies

Assume, for this example only, that there are no resistances, fortifications or special abilities:

  • Enemy A has Attack 3 and Armour 4.
  • Enemy B has Attack 4 and Armour 3.
  • You can produce Ranged Attack 3, Block 3, and regular Attack 4.
  1. Ranged/Siege: target Enemy B with Ranged Attack 3. It meets Armour 3, so B is defeated and removed before it can attack.
  2. Block: Enemy A is still attacking. Block 3 meets its Attack 3, so its attack is stopped completely.
  3. Assign Damage: Enemy A is blocked, so it deals no damage.
  4. Attack: regular Attack 4 meets Enemy A's Armour 4, defeating it.

The example illustrates why phase order matters: defeating or fully blocking an enemy before Assign Damage prevents its attack from dealing damage. In a real fight, check all enemy abilities and resistances before copying the arithmetic.
