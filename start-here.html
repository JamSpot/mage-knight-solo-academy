YOUR LEARNING ROUTE

Start here: your first Mage Knight game

Follow this route in order. Learn one layer at a time, play the introductory scenario, then move to solo conquest and expansion content. You do not need to learn every rule before your first turn.

Learning route v1

Use the rules for your edition. This academy is written primarily for Mage Knight: Ultimate Edition. WizKids states that Ultimate Edition includes the base game and all three board-game expansions: The Lost Legion, Krang, and Shades of Tezla. It does not include unrelated Mage Knight miniatures-game products.

Step 1 — Put the right books beside you

Open the official rule-book library and use the Ultimate Edition Walkthrough for your first play, with the Ultimate Edition Rules available for clarification. The walkthrough is designed to teach by playing through examples, rather than requiring you to memorise the full rulebook first.

Open official Ultimate Edition walkthrough

Step 2 — Play “The First Reconnaissance”

This is the official introductory scenario. Use its exact setup, component selection, map instructions, round limit and ending rules. Follow the walkthrough in order and pause at each example to perform the same action with your own cards. Do not improvise the scenario setup from a different edition's guide.

  • Read the scenario's objective and end condition before setup.
  • Sort out only the components requested for this scenario.
  • Build the map and prepare decks exactly as the walkthrough specifies.
  • Keep the walkthrough open at the relevant section during play.
  • When a rule is unclear, check the official Ultimate Edition rules rather than guessing.
  • After the game, note the two or three rules that slowed you down most.

Step 3 — Learn these systems in this order

  1. Turn and round structure. Know whose turn it is, what changes between Day and Night, and what causes the round or scenario to end.
  2. Playing cards. Practise basic effects first, then powered effects and how mana enables them.
  3. Movement and exploration. Read terrain costs carefully; plan the destination and the action you want to take there before spending movement.
  4. Combat. Separate ranged/siege attacks, blocking, attack, damage absorption, wounds and rewards. They are related but not interchangeable.
  5. Advancement and deck growth. Learn how fame, levels, skills, units and improved cards change your options.
  6. Locations and special rules. Add site-specific rules as you encounter them instead of trying to memorise every location in advance.

Step 4 — Use this routine on every turn

  1. Check the scenario clock and objective.
  2. Choose one main goal for this turn.
  3. Check the route, terrain and any site or enemy rules that could affect the plan.
  4. Work out whether your hand can pay for the movement and action you need.
  5. Before committing to combat, calculate the whole sequence and the likely cost in wounds or unit losses.
  6. Resolve the turn, update the map and trackers, then choose the next priority.

Step 5 — Move to solo conquest

Once the introductory scenario feels comfortable, use the official rulebook's Solo Conquest setup and victory conditions. This is the next step for learning the full strategic game. The solo rules are scenario-specific: do not assume the introductory scenario's dummy-player or ending procedure carries over unchanged.

Continue to the solo play guide

Step 6 — Add the collection gradually

  • Base game first: become comfortable with a full Solo Conquest game and its scoring.
  • The Lost Legion: then learn its additional cards, units, Volkare material and scenarios from the official expansion rules.
  • Krang: learn the character-specific content and any card or skill text that changes your choices.
  • Shades of Tezla: add its faction material, Braevalar and scenario-specific rules when you are ready.

Ultimate Edition integrates the expansion collection, but that does not mean every expansion mechanism belongs in your first learning game. Add content in deliberate stages and follow the selected scenario's component list.

Step 7 — Keep these references handy

  • Academy rules library — topic index and planned worked examples.
  • Scenario and expansion index — what to learn next.
  • Solo play guide — solo planning and session routine.
  • Official rule-book library — primary rules and walkthrough PDFs.
  • WizKids Ultimate Edition product page — confirms the edition's included content.
Accuracy promise: this is a learning route, not a replacement for the official rules. Where exact timing, scenario setup or an exception matters, follow the Ultimate Edition rulebook and the chosen scenario. The detailed scenario walkthroughs and worked combat examples are still being built and checked.
