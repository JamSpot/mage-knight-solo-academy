SOLO ACADEMY

Learn to play solo

Use this after the introductory scenario in Start here. Build from the official Solo Conquest rules into a reliable turn-planning routine.

Guide in development
First principle: choose one scenario and use only the rules and components it calls for. Do not add expansion rules just because the components are available.

1. Choose a learning scenario

For a first solo session, choose between two sensible routes. The official First Reconnaissance solo variant is shorter and teaches the mechanics without requiring you to conquer cities. If you already understand the basics, move to Solo Conquest, the full six-round solo mission.

Use the Start Here learning route and the official Ultimate Edition walkthrough for your first play.

2. First Reconnaissance — solo variant

The Ultimate Edition scenario book specifies this adjustment to the introductory scenario:

  • Use 8 countryside tiles, as in the two-player setup.
  • Play four rounds: two Day rounds and two Night rounds.
  • Use a Dummy player, and use the Tactic-selection special rules from Solo Conquest.
  • At scoring, award no titles.

If you only want to learn the mechanics and the dummy player feels like too much at once, the official rules also allow you to ignore the Dummy player for your first learning game. Keep the objective and scenario timing from the scenario book.

3. Solo Conquest — verified setup reference

These are the core setup settings in the official Ultimate Edition rulebook. Use the scenario book and its full setup instructions alongside this summary.

SettingSolo Conquest rule
LengthSix rounds: three Day rounds and three Night rounds.
Map shapeWedge.
Map tilesSeven countryside tiles, two Core city tiles and two Core non-city tiles.
City levelsFirst city revealed is level 5; second is level 8.
Dummy playerUse one standard Dummy player.
Remove from decksRemove the four competitive Spells numbered 109–112, and remove the interactive Skill marked by the competitive icon from your Skill deck.

Solo Conquest: special timing rules

  1. You choose your Tactic first; the Dummy player then takes one random Tactic from those remaining.
  2. At the end of each Day or Night, remove both Tactics used that round from the game. Each Tactic is used only once during this scenario.
  3. When all cities have been conquered, you take one final turn. The Dummy player does not.

Solo Conquest: scoring checklist

First determine whether you defeated both cities: that is the mission's success condition. Then calculate the score using the official Scoring card and rulebook:

  • Start with your Fame and apply standard Achievements scoring, but award no titles.
  • Add 10 points for each city you conquered, plus 15 points if you conquered both.
  • Add 30 points for each full round remaining before the six-round limit when you finished.
  • Add 1 point for each card remaining in the Dummy player's Deed deck that has not been flipped this round.
  • Add 5 points if you had not announced the End of the Round in your final round.
Do not mix scenario rules: Solo Conquest's six-round limit, two city levels and scoring are not the same as First Reconnaissance. Always check the selected scenario before setup.

2. Solo session checklist

  • Confirm the scenario, player count, difficulty and time limit.
  • Separate the correct decks, tiles, tokens and units before setup.
  • Set up the dummy player exactly as that scenario instructs.
  • Read the end condition and scoring before taking the first turn.
  • Track rounds and any scenario-specific dummy-player progress visibly.
  • After each round, check the current objective before planning the next one.

4. A repeatable turn-planning routine

  1. Check the clock: how many rounds remain, and what must be achieved before time runs out?
  2. Set a priority: explore, recruit, gain experience, improve the deck, or prepare for a major fight.
  3. Read the map: estimate movement cost and consider terrain, site effects and likely threats.
  4. Sequence cards: use the available cards to meet movement, influence, attack, block or healing needs efficiently.
  5. Price the risk: compare the reward with wounds, lost turns, unit casualties and resource cost.
  6. End with a plan: know what the next turn is trying to accomplish.

5. Beginner priorities

  • Learn the difference between basic and powered card effects.
  • Do not assume every enemy must be fought; the scenario objective should guide your route.
  • Value efficient deck use and sensible rest timing, not only immediate fame.
  • Recruitment can provide persistent capabilities, but consider command limits and the unit's role.
  • Before a major assault, calculate the full attack, block, damage and wound sequence.
